Part I · Motivation

Garbage in, garbage out — even for deep learning

What a model really learns

The statistical patterns in its training data — including the errors, gaps and biases.

model = data + algorithm

What bad air-quality data does

  • Stuck sensor → a flat line the model believes
  • CO in mg/m³ at one station → fake “clean air”
  • Gaps during the worst smog → biased sample
  • Random split of hourly data → fake accuracy

What good preparation buys

  • Higher, honest accuracy
  • Faster training
  • Models that work next winter
  • Trust from environmental experts

Part I · Sampling

Population, sample — and the bias between them

Population vs sample

The population is the air everyone in Beijing breathes, every hour. Our data is a sample: 12 fixed sites over 4 years. A model generalises only to what the sample represents.

Biases to watch

  • Siting — urban core vs suburban stations
  • Downtime — monitors fail during extreme episodes
  • Temporal — 2013–2017 spans major clean-air policies
  • Instrument — calibration changes

What to do

  • Document how data was collected
  • Compare train vs test distributions
  • Split by time; test on unseen stations
🎙 No amount of cleaning fixes a sample that does not represent the problem.

Part II · Relationships

Correlation — useful, but handle with care

Pearson $r$

Linear association, $-1\le r\le 1$. Sensitive to outliers and skew.

Spearman $\rho$

Rank-based, monotonic association. Robust — the better default for pollutants.

Watch out

  • Correlation ≠ causation
  • Shared seasonal cycles create correlation
  • Autocorrelation: hour $t$ ≈ hour $t+1$
PM2.5 ↔ PM10, CO: strongly positivewind speed ↔ PM2.5: negative (dispersion)O3 ↔ NO2: often negative (chemistry)
🎙 Physics sanity check: does the sign make atmospheric sense?

Part III · Outliers — detect

Four ways to find an outlier in sensor data

z-score / robust z

$|x-\bar x|/s>3$ — or with median & MAD, which the outliers cannot inflate.

IQR rule (Tukey)

Outside $[Q_1-1.5\,\text{IQR},\;Q_3+1.5\,\text{IQR}]$. Flags many points in skewed data — apply on log scale.

Rolling (Hampel)

Compare each hour with its rolling median ± 3·MAD → catches spikes and flat lines.

Physics & domain

PM2.5 ≤ PM10 · concentrations ≥ 0 · plausible pressure · no −999 codes.

The strongest test.

Put features on a comparable scale

Standardisation

$z=(x-\mu)/\sigma$ → mean 0, std 1.

Default for linear models, SVM, NN, PCA.

Min-Max

$x'=(x-x_{min})/(x_{max}-x_{min})$ → [0,1].

Bounded inputs; sensitive to outliers.

$(x-\text{median})/\text{IQR}$.

Part IV · Partitioning

Split by time — hour t and hour t+1 are near-twins

Random split ✗

Neighbouring hours land in train and test; the model “interpolates” and the score is inflated.

Time-based hold-out ✓

Train Mar 2013 – Feb 2016, test on the final 12 months — a full seasonal cycle, used once.

TimeSeriesSplit CV ✓

Expanding window: each fold trains on the past, validates on the next block.

Station hold-out ✓

Leave one or more stations out → does the model work at a new site?

autocorrelation is the reasonduplicates across the split = leakage

A leak-free preprocessing pipeline

num = Pipeline([("impute", SimpleImputer(strategy="median", add_indicator=True)),
                ("scale", StandardScaler())])
cat = Pipeline([("impute", SimpleImputer(strategy="most_frequent")),
                ("onehot", OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore"))])
prep = ColumnTransformer([("num", num, NUM_COLS), ("cat", cat, ["station"])])
model = Pipeline([("prep", prep), ("reg", HistGradientBoostingRegressor())])
